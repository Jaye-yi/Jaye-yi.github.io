FA-ReID

Separate identity evidence from changing appearance

FA-ReID combines RGB appearance with human-parsing structure, using multi-scale cross-attention and adversarial learning to build identity representations that remain useful when clothing changes.

The problem: clothing is visible but not reliable

Conventional person re-identification systems can depend heavily on clothing color and texture. When the same person changes clothes, those visually dominant cues become misleading, while more stable identity evidence is harder to isolate.

FA-ReID addresses this setting by combining appearance information with body-structure information and explicitly reducing clothing dependence in the learned representation.

Align appearance with structural evidence

The published method uses a dual-stream architecture: one branch processes RGB images and the other processes multi-channel human-parsing maps. A Multi-Scale Cross-Attention module uses structural features to guide the RGB stream toward identity-relevant regions and away from misleading clothing texture.

  • RGB stream for global appearance information.
  • Human-parsing stream for clothing-agnostic body-part structure.
  • Multi-scale cross-attention for guided feature alignment across the two streams.

Suppress clothing information adversarially

The framework adds a clothing-stripping adversarial objective built around a gradient-reversal layer and entropy maximization. The goal is to make clothing categories difficult to recover from the identity representation, encouraging the network to retain cues that transfer across outfits.

Evaluation and publication

The paper evaluates FA-ReID on PRCC, LTCC, and VC-Clothes, three clothing-changing person re-identification benchmarks. The published article reports state-of-the-art performance in its evaluation and presents ablations for the feature-alignment and adversarial components.

The article appears in Neurocomputing, volume 666, article 132237, dated February 2026; the DOI and my CV retain the 2025 online-publication context.

My contribution and connection to my current work

I am a coauthor, and the publisher’s CRediT statement records my contribution as writing – original draft. I keep that role distinct from the methodology and experiments credited to the collaboration as a whole.

Although this project concerns person re-identification rather than robotics, it sharpened a question that continues in my current research: which visual evidence is stable enough to support a decision when an obvious appearance cue changes?

Outcomes

  • Peer-reviewed publication in Neurocomputing, volume 666, article 132237.
  • A dual-stream and adversarial framework evaluated on three clothing-changing re-identification benchmarks.

Why a coffee workstation?

Coffee preparation brings several sources of uncertainty into one familiar workspace: object pose, visibility, graspability, contact, and dependencies between steps. A small local error can affect a later action.

I use this setting to turn abstract questions about actionable belief into concrete engineering decisions: what to inspect, what to calibrate, what the controller can reliably execute, and which assumption to revisit after a mismatch.

What I am building

  • A 3D-printed Arm-200 platform that I assembled independently.
  • Camera calibration and perception for objects and task-relevant relations.
  • Control and manipulation routines connected through closed-loop tests.
  • An experimental workflow for relating observed action outcomes to uncertain spatial, affordance, or physical assumptions.

Progress and research scope

The physical platform is assembled. I am developing and integrating the coffee-making system; I do not yet present it as a validated autonomous coffee-preparation system.

My intended experiments compare the value of another view, a low-risk interaction, and proceeding with the current plan. Execution feedback could then help distinguish a perception error from an unsuitable contact assumption or a controller limitation.

Interaction design matters here too

A home robot will share space, objects, and attention with a person. A useful intervention should be understandable and proportionate to the uncertainty. I want the system to communicate what it needs, rather than repeatedly requesting generic help.

That question connects the physical testbed to my experience designing human-facing interfaces. The archived mobile UX prototypes are a separate interaction-design project, linked below.

Outcomes

  • An independently assembled Arm-200 platform.
  • An ongoing integration effort spanning calibration, perception, control, and closed-loop evaluation.

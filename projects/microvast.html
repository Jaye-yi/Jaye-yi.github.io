Robot safety in shared indoor spaces

The project considered how humanoid and other embodied robots could operate more safely around people during manipulation, housekeeping, and indoor mobility. The research question was how to recognize potentially unsafe behavior and support intervention while retaining useful task execution.

Robot-arm tasks provided an initial setting for the experimental workflow. This public overview describes the application and my research scope rather than the technical implementation.

Limited public disclosure. Technical details are withheld due to patent-related confidentiality.

My scope

I helped turn a manipulation-research direction into an experimental setup: research planning, lab setup, an initial prototype, simulation experiments, and tests on physical robot arms.

At Microvast’s innovation center, I led learning-based manipulation work on algorithms and systems. I developed research plans, helped establish the experimental lab, and built an initial prototype.

From algorithm to experiment

  • Planned the research workflow and experimental setup.
  • Ran simulation experiments and designed supporting devices.
  • Tested prototypes on physical robot arms.

What changed in my thinking

Physical execution gives a researcher evidence that a simulator can hide: calibration, contact, sensing, and hardware limitations interact. An unexpected outcome rarely identifies its own cause.

This experience sharpened my interest in systems that diagnose which belief or component failed, then acquire the evidence needed to update it.

Outcomes

  • Research plans, experimental infrastructure, an initial prototype, and simulation/real-arm testing.

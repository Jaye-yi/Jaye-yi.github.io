Offer a route and room to wander

The project centered on a city-guide system with two modes. In the guided mode, players could go sightseeing through prepared animations. In the free mode, they could move around, play minigames, and interact with non-player characters.

I built both modes and their interface in Unity3D. This made navigation part of the creative brief: the same virtual world needed to support a sequence chosen by its maker and activities chosen by its player.

Build the world as well as its controls

My work also included modeling and rendering characters, underwater scenes, vegetation, and creatures in Maya. The environment was a substantial part of the project, alongside the code and interface that allowed someone to move through it.

I find the relationship between these tasks especially interesting in retrospect. A scene can invite attention through its visual detail, while the navigation mode determines how much control the player has over following that interest. Designing both modes made that balance between guidance and agency concrete.

Package the experience

I packaged a Windows executable and a mobile version that operated with Google Cardboard and an iPhone. The project let me work through the full chain from scene assets and interaction logic to a navigable experience on a device.

I remain interested in the same interaction question in robotics: when should a system guide a person, and when should it leave room for them to choose?

Outcomes

  • I implemented guided sightseeing and free exploration with minigames and non-player-character interactions.
  • I created Maya scene assets and packaged desktop and mobile VR versions.

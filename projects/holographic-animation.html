Bring an animation off the ordinary screen

I developed this as an independent interactive design project. The starting material was a dancing 3D figure; the ambition was to present the animation through a projection pyramid on an iPad and connect that presentation with an interface.

I handled the animation, interface, and physical setup myself. That scope let me work across the full chain from creating moving material to arranging how someone would encounter it.

Connect three kinds of making

I modeled and created the dance animation with MikuMikuDance, built the interface in Unity3D, and operated the projection-pyramid display on an iPad. Each tool served a different part of the same concept: character motion, an interface layer, and a physical viewing arrangement.

The iPad played front, back, left, and right views of the MMD animation in a four-direction layout. I 3D-printed a support that held an upright, dark transparent plastic pyramid over the screen.

The projection pyramid was part of the presentation itself. I was working with both the animated figure and the device arrangement used to show it. The concrete output was a dance animation presented through that specific iPad setup, with its interface built in Unity.

Reflect on the display as an interface

Looking back, I am interested in how the screen controls and the apparent position of the figure relate to one another. Where should attention go when the interface sits on a device but the animation appears in the display above it? The project brought my attention to the relationship between digital feedback, physical arrangement, and a viewer’s expectations.

Outcomes

  • I created a 3D dance animation and a Unity interface for the independent project.
  • I assembled and operated an iPad projection-pyramid presentation of the concept.
